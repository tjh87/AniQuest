# AniQuest: current Singapore animal catalogue

Revision: **14 September 2026**. These figures count AniQuest records, not Singapore wildlife populations or the total number of animal species in Singapore.

AniQuest contains **141 complete profiles**, **282 sourced fun facts**, **141 distinct credited reference photographs** and six automatically generated learning checks for every profile. The current scope is vertebrates: mammals, birds, reptiles and amphibians. Fish and insects remain outside scope.

| Group | Complete profiles |
| --- | ---: |
| Mammals | 29 |
| Birds | 91 |
| Reptiles | 14 |
| Amphibians | 7 |
| **Total** | **141** |

Of these, **134 are native** and seven are introduced. The native count includes natural migrants and species with both native and introduced Singapore populations. The catalogue is a growing, reviewed learning collection; it is not a complete inventory of every native Singapore animal.

Every profile includes common and scientific names, aliases, family, origin, habitats, a recognition guide, diet, activity, behaviour, reproduction, Singapore occurrence, ecological role, pressures, responsible observation guidance, qualitative encounter information, national conservation status, global-status evidence state, references and two fun facts. Unknown evidence is labelled rather than guessed.

## Conservation coverage

National categories come from the Singapore Red Data Book 3 tables published by NParks. Encounter labels such as “commonly seen” describe visibility and never override the conservation category.

## Common-animal expansion

The first common-animal batch adds 16 previously missing animals, each with full profile fields, two linked fun facts, a credited photograph and a six-check lesson (96 additional learning checks).

- Native birds: yellow-vented bulbul, Asian glossy starling, common tailorbird, spotted dove, zebra dove, pink-necked green pigeon, brown-throated sunbird, scarlet-backed flowerpecker, common iora, white-breasted waterhen and white-throated kingfisher.
- Native reptiles and amphibians: common sun skink and field frog.
- Introduced animals: rock pigeon, changeable lizard and Asian toad. These remain clearly distinguished from native wildlife.

The Asian toad follows the name *Duttaphrynus bengalensis* in the [NParks amphibian RDB3 table](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/nature/species-list/amphibians). The [BiodiversitySG account](https://biodiversitysg.nparks.gov.sg/our-biodiversity/amphibians/common-asian-toad/) uses *D. melanostictus*. Both names find one profile, with an explicit source-naming note. Current NParks sources classify this animal as introduced. The changeable lizard's national category remains unverified; its common encounter frequency is not a conservation assessment.

Bird identification and Singapore occurrence use [Bird Society of Singapore](https://singaporebirds.com/). Additional reproduction evidence links to NParks, Animal Diversity Web, BirdLife Australia and explicitly regional field observations from the Bird Ecology Study Group. Overseas observations are not used to invent Singapore breeding dates.

## Second common-animal expansion

The second batch adds nine resident birds, two reptiles and one amphibian: ornate sunbird, Eurasian tree sparrow, scaly-breasted munia, Pacific swallow, Sunda pied fantail, ashy tailorbird, olive-winged bulbul, Oriental magpie-robin, common flameback, Oriental whip snake, green paddy frog and red-eared slider.

The scaly-breasted munia and Oriental magpie-robin profiles explain their mixed native and introduced Singapore records. The tree sparrow repeats the question mark attached to origin in the NParks table. The ornate sunbird retains the current split name but leaves its exact national category unverified because RDB3 lists the former broader taxon. The red-eared slider is introduced and has no invented RDB3 category.

## Migratory-bird expansion

Nine complete migrant profiles are now included: Common sandpiper, Barn swallow, Asian brown flycatcher, Arctic warbler, Brown shrike, Blue-tailed bee-eater, Pacific golden plover, Common redshank and Yellow-rumped flycatcher.

Each profile links its Bird Society of Singapore species account and eBird-derived weekly chart. The seasonal text gives approximate chart periods and states that they are not fixed annual arrival dates. Breeding information describes the species in its northern breeding range and does not claim breeding in Singapore.

The clickable **Migratory** tag now appears on these nine profiles and 21 earlier migrant profiles. Selecting the tag filters the catalogue to all 30 tagged animals.

## Global assessment handling

AniQuest keeps Singapore categories and global assessments separate. Ninety-four profiles retain a dated or explicitly scoped global reference. The ten mammal, 28 common-animal and nine migratory-bird profiles added on 14 September say “Dated global assessment not verified in this edition” until a taxon-matched current assessment is reviewed. A Singapore RDB3 label is never copied into the global field.

## Images and learning

Every profile has a distinct real reference photograph with a source-page credit. A photograph taken outside Singapore is labelled as a reference image and does not imply that the pictured individual was seen locally. Classic, Pixelated and Book themes preserve the natural photograph instead of pixelating, recolouring or cropping it. Book uses serif headings, page edges, running page labels and bookmark navigation in light and dark modes.

Each profile automatically receives a six-question guided lesson from `app/species-lessons.ts`. The catalogue now provides 846 guided checks. Older lesson IDs and versions are preserved.

## Calendar coverage

The local calendar contains **37 confirmed dated sessions** from August through **26 December 2026**: one in August, 16 in September, 13 in October, four in November and three in December. Every added nature session has a direct organiser page, SGT start and end time, location, category and either a published registration deadline or an explicit registration-status note. Undated programmes remain outside the dated calendar.

## Principal implementation files

- `app/species-data.ts` assembles the full catalogue and conservation filters.
- `app/native-species-expansion.ts` contains the ten new full profiles.
- `app/common-species-expansion.ts` contains the 16 common-animal profiles.
- `app/additional-common-species.ts` contains the second batch of 12 common-animal profiles.
- `app/migratory-bird-expansion.ts` contains nine complete migrant profiles and their seasonal evidence.
- `app/profile-photos.ts` stores distinct image credits and source pages.
- `app/species-lessons.ts` creates six guided checks per profile.
- `app/animal-events-data.ts` stores confirmed dated calendar sessions and ICS exports.
- `app/content-reviews.json` stores claim-level source-review records.
- `docs/editorial/reviews/native-species-expansion-2026-09-14.json` is the expansion audit batch.

Use the site for the full profile text and references. Future expansion should proceed in reviewed batches from NParks taxon lists, with a complete profile, two sourced facts, a distinct credited photograph and claim-level review for every added animal.
