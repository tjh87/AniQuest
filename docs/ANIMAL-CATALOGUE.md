# AniQuest: current Singapore animal catalogue

Revision: **6 September 2026**. These are counts of included AniQuest records, not Singapore wildlife totals or population estimates.

**49 full profiles, 98 animal fun facts, 3 embedded real photographs.** Other profiles provide source links to reference pictures. No insect or fish profiles are included. “Fish owl” is a bird name and remains in scope.

| Group | Profiles |
| --- | ---: |
| Mammal | 19 |
| Bird | 16 |
| Reptile | 10 |

Every profile includes recognition, scientific/common names and aliases, family, origin, habitats, diet, activity, behaviour, reproduction, Singapore occurrence, ecological role, pressures, safe viewing, qualitative encounter information, conservation information, sources and two fun facts. Unknown evidence is labelled instead of guessed.

## Conservation coverage

| Endangered (EN) | 8 |
| Critically Endangered (CR) | 13 |
| Not Applicable (NA) | 2 |
| Not assessed here (editorial UNV) | 1 |
| Total | 45 |

The **25 nationally threatened records** are the union of VU, EN and CR. The catalogue's threatened filter uses that definition; it does not include NT. Exact category filters and the chart use the same records. The Banded bullfrog's “Not assessed here” is an editorial evidence gap, not an official Red Data Book category: no matching row appears in the [amphibian table checked for this review](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/nature/species-list/amphibians/). Its [NParks species account](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/florafaunaweb/fauna/4/5/455) records introduced origin and common occurrence.

There are **42 native and 3 introduced records**. Javan myna and house crow are nationally NA. Introduced origin does not imply a species is globally safe, and frequent sightings do not invalidate a threatened category. Long-tailed macaque is nationally LC but its recovered global reference is EN; Javan myna is introduced/NA locally but VU in its dated global reference.

## Naming and source corrections

- Use *Pelobatrachus nasutus* for Malayan horned frog, retaining *Megophrys nasuta* for search. The misspelling in the NParks RDB3 table is explained, not adopted as the accepted name.
- Keep *Varanus nebulosus* separate from the broader *Varanus bengalensis* concept used in some older sources.
- Keep *Paradoxurus musangus* separate from the older broad *P. hermaphroditus* global concept. Common palm civet remains an alias.
- Current RDB3 categories supersede older labels on species pages, including buffy fish owl, blue-eared kingfisher, changeable hawk-eagle, spiny hill turtle, Malayan horned frog and black-eyed litter frog.
- Source-card names identify the actual publisher resource instead of calling every NParks page Flora & Fauna Web.

## Image limits and content files

Embedded photographs show Sunda colugo, Oriental pied hornbill and smooth-coated otters. The remaining 42 profiles use linked reference pictures. The supplied logo is branding and must not be used to identify animals. Classic/Retro and light/dark styling do not alter the real photos.

- `app/species-data.ts`: joined current catalogue, names, national categories and search/filter logic.
- `app/species-profiles.ts`: detailed profiles and facts for the previous 30 animals.
- `app/species-expansion.ts`: records, full profiles and evidence for the 15 additions.
- `app/species-assessments.ts`: recovered dated global references for nine existing animals.
- `app/animal-atlas.tsx`: catalogue, profile tabs, source panels and playful fact display.
- `docs/research/`: research records and audit observations, with source links and limits.

Use the app for full profile and fact text. Update this catalogue when source records change, and consult `PLAN-REVIEW.md` for actual release-test results.
